# Candy Cruisers V2.0.0

Unity rebuild in the original `john-thomas-gray/color-chameleon` repository.
The previous version remains available at the `legacy-v1` tag and in the local
`/Users/johngray/swe/candy-cruisers-v1-archive` checkout. The active project is
`/Users/johngray/swe/candy-cruisers`. See [Changelog](CHANGELOG.md).

New rows contain at most three distinct colors. Each color appears in one
adjacent run, with randomized run sizes and color order. Rows spawn five enemies
wide through level 6, then six enemies wide from level 7 onward. This applies to
opening fleets, new top rows, and refill batches, not individual Purple summons
or Yellow transformations.

Open this folder with Unity 6000.3.24f1, open `Assets/Scenes/Gameplay.unity`, and press Play.

The main menu opens first over a solid black background. Choose Play or
press Enter to start. Use P, Escape or the pause button to pause/resume; pause
and game-over menus offer Restart and Main Menu. Game over records positive
scores to a local five-entry leaderboard, which is also shown on the main menu.
Focus loss pauses the run until you resume it.
See [Menus and Pause](docs/MENUS_AND_PAUSE.md).

Character artwork lives under `Visuals` children. Firing, matching and defeat
have replaceable cue-prefab slots and animation hooks on `CharacterVisuals`.
See [Art Replacement](docs/ART_REPLACEMENT.md) for the artist-facing workflow.

The tongue has a continuous chiptune slide-whistle whose pitch follows its visible
length throughout extension and retraction, including slow deflected returns.
The reusable sound service supports shared volume/mute, pause, and replaceable tones.
Gameplay music recordings are local-only: keep full audio files in `Assets/Resources`
when playtesting, but Git ignores common music formats and their Unity metadata.
Clean clones run silently while retaining beat maps, loudness metadata and music-player code.
See [Sound Effects](docs/SOUND_EFFECTS.md) for tuning and adding cues.

Edit ability cooldowns in `Assets/Scripts/CombatBalance.cs`, in `CooldownRange`.
Each `Vector2` holds the minimum and maximum in seconds; Red uses the `default`
case. The function then multiplies both by 0.75 and adjusts the maximum by level,
with a floor of minimum + 3 seconds. Source values are authoritative when tuning.

For spawn testing, open **Candy Cruisers > Spawn Overrides**. Enable **Override level eligibility**, then check the enemy types you want. These selections replace level unlocks for the next opening fleet, future rows, refill batches, and Purple summons; they do not change existing enemies or Yellow imitation. At least one type must remain selected. Turn the override off to restore normal unlocks. Settings survive Play mode and scene restarts within the current editor session, and the control is excluded from player builds.

The gameplay scene starts empty against a solid black background. After one musical opening beat, ten enemies arrive in two rows of five, using Red/Blue unless a development override is active. Every arriving enemy fades in through a color-matched electric rift over 0.8 seconds, including new rows, refill batches, and Purple summons. After the rift finishes, Blue shields expand from a point in front of the enemy over 0.35 seconds. Their bright near-white cyan arcs become protective when fully powered. The formation reverses and descends only when its leading occupied column touches the playfield border. Empty edge columns allow more travel. Select **Enemy Grid** to adjust **Speed**; fixed Travel Distance is no longer used. The logical grid tracks sixty-six cells, unique enemy identities, occupied neighbors, and available colors.

Move with Left/Right arrows or A/D; fire with Space. On touch screens, drag horizontally to move and release a short tap to fire. Mouse dragging/clicking provides the same gesture controls for desktop testing. Movement wraps at the playfield edges; pointer movement takes the shortest wrapped route. The player's body shows the shot color. One tongue can be active at a time, and it follows the player while extending and retracting. The next color is chosen from eligible colors, weighted by all on-screen enemies of each color and their row depth, with lower enemies contributing more. A continuous special-Blue barrier across the occupied formation, including diagonal and mixed paths, limits selection to Blue or enemies below that barrier and doubles Blue's weight. An empty grid disables firing.

The extending tongue passes through mismatched enemies. Its first matching hit clears that enemy and its connected same-color group through horizontal and vertical neighbors; diagonal neighbors do not connect. It then retracts from the hit position, without clearing more enemies on return. Hit detection checks the tip's full vertical travel each frame against current sprite bounds.

When retraction starts, the tongue previews the player's next color. The player adopts that reserved color on return. An earned magic charge gives the returning tongue a rainbow preview; spending magic without earning another charge restores a single-color return. The preview also follows reserved next-wave colors and rechecks colors that become unreachable during the return.

The combo multiplier sits beneath Level at the top right. Shot feedback displays the score calculation, such as `200 x3 = +600`, and carries already-awarded points forward when later hits use a higher multiplier, such as `300 + 100 x4 = +700`. Fleet bonuses are added separately. A missed shot or player hit sends the lost multiplier to screen center, where it cracks into two halves and fades. Scoring resets immediately; the break animation pauses with gameplay.

At each occupied-column edge contact, survivors descend one cell and random level-eligible enemies spawn in the top row: five enemies through level 6, then six enemies from level 7 onward. With one Green, all five early-row columns occupied, and level-one speed, the first contact occurs after 50 seconds and subsequent contacts every 75 seconds. More Greens increase speed proportionally; zero Greens stop horizontal movement entirely. Level-one fleets therefore stay still until Green unlocks and actually spawns. Clearing outer columns delays contact. Columns and the grid origin are relabeled together when necessary so survivors do not jump sideways and a full new row stays inside the border. An occupied bottom row prevents additional descent and spawning, but the fleet keeps sweeping. The eleventh row reaches player height; only actual enemy-body contact in that row ends the run. Both player and fleet movement check the whole traveled segment, including fast moves, while wrapping does not count as crossing the middle of the field. Movement, retreat, player input, enemy abilities, and missiles stop, and a Game Over overlay offers Restart. Press R to restart at any time, or use the button after game over. Restart resets player state and timers, repeats the empty opening pause, then phases in the 10-enemy formation.

See [Occupied-column movement](docs/OCCUPIED_COLUMN_MOVEMENT.md) for the geometry, update sequence, row-placement strategy, and verification details.

Clearing the entire fleet pauses the fleet for one second, preserves fired missiles, resets the fleet's position and sweep timer, and spawns a fresh batch: 10 enemies at levels 1-2, 15 at level 3, 25 at levels 4-6, and 36 thereafter. Player movement and edge wrapping continue throughout this pause, including an ongoing touch or mouse drag. Firing waits for enemies to return. Select **Enemy Grid > Game Session** to adjust the refill delay.

The score and level display includes a nine-pixel-tall level-progress bar (three times its original height) and brief score/unlock feedback. Each defeated enemy awards `100 + 10 * (level - 1)` points before death-digit, magic and combo multipliers; a cleared fleet adds `10000 * level`. Every distinct color destroyed by a shot immediately adds one combo streak point after that contact scores. Magic shots use the increased multiplier on their next contact, without awarding the same color twice in one shot. A basic Yellow's linked group counts both destroyed colors, for regular shots too. Returning adds no extra point; a miss or player hit resets the streak. Cumulative defeat thresholds are 18, 54, 108, 180, 270, and so on. Red/Blue are available from level 1, Green at 2, Yellow at 4, and Purple at 6. These gates apply to rows, refills, and summons. Game over shows final score; restart resets progression. The background remains solid black across wave and level changes. See [Level Progression](docs/LEVEL_PROGRESSION.md) for the dedicated level-by-level design reference.

Each live Green contributes 0.03 world units per second before level scaling. Removing or converting the last Green stops the fleet. The earlier dash implementation remains available through **Enemy Grid > Use Green Dashes**, off by default. Purple preserves the original fleet-wide gap spawning, with a brilliant flash and electric rift around the incoming enemy. Yellow attaches to a random non-Yellow orthogonal neighbor for two seconds before adopting its type. During that transition, Yellow remains matchable as Yellow, and clearing either side also clears the attached Yellow and the target's connected color group. Killing the last Yellow earns its clear bar; transformation alone never does and does not block future Yellow spawns. If the last Yellow finishes transforming while the player's selected color is Yellow, the player takes a normal hit. See [Progression and abilities](docs/PROGRESSION_AND_ABILITIES.md) for exact timing and replacement contracts.

A minimalist arcade-style frame marks the playfield: a stronger inner boundary, chamfered outer outline, and bright corner brackets. Every line follows the player's displayed color, including magic flashing. The inner edges still mark the same wrap and fleet-turn boundaries; all decorations fit portrait and landscape screens.

Descent checks cover sparse formations, identity and color preservation, exactly one row per turnaround, long-frame boundary crossings, and full-grid blocking. The full suite also checks exactly-once fleet clears, batch sizing, fresh sweep timing, and a complete Play mode sequence through refill, game over, and scene restart.

Matching checks cover cyclic groups, isolated and diagonal enemies, full-fleet clears, nearest matching hits behind mismatched enemies, moving formations, and frame-rate independence. Play mode also verifies a matching chain clear and subsequent firing.

Red enemies fire straight downward missiles on independent beat-aligned cooldowns, with a bright flash in the final 0.45 seconds before firing. Missiles survive their shooter and fleet clears, keep moving and can hit the player during refill. They fly offscreen without wrapping, and their proximity-based flight audio follows their actual position. The descending slide and air sound becomes steeply louder nearby, drops significantly after passing the player, and is silent beyond the configured hearing range; side, bottom and lifetime cleanup allow missiles to become inaudible before disappearing. Impacts still retire them immediately. Pause and game over suspend them; restarting or returning to the main menu clears them with the scene. The player starts each run with three spare lives in addition to the active player, so four deaths end the run without a refill. Three miniature players replace the Lives readout and follow the player's color and beat. After each recoverable death, one miniature grows and explodes before respawn. A normal hit hides and disables the player for 1.5 seconds, cancels the tongue, then respawns with another 1.5 seconds of flashing protection. The final hit plays the player death cue and opens game over. Filling every bottom color-clear bar grants one extra life, capped at three spares.

Ordinary Blue enemies start without shields. Their initial cooldown begins at spawn, using the same 2.25-7.5-second level-one starting range as recharging; only after that cooldown do they perform the 0.35-second shield power-up. Yellows that become ordinary Blues also start on cooldown. A mismatched tongue is absorbed by the nearest active ordinary shield and retracts, breaking it and restarting its cooldown. A Blue tongue bypasses the shield and can clear the Blue enemy normally. While the shield is down, mismatched tongues pass through. Upgraded Blue group shields retain their existing arrival/promotion behavior. New rows receive the same abilities, and defeated enemies stop using them.

Connected same-color groups of three or more promote to tier two for all five colors, shown as downward-pointing equilateral triangles. Upgraded Blues wrap their connected Blue group in a rigid perimeter shield: a mismatched ordinary shot turns the player grey and stuns movement until the tongue returns. Blue and magic shots bypass it. Upgraded Reds turn toward the player during their warning, fire a straight aimed missile, then turn back. Upgraded Greens add the retained short fast-forward dash to count-based movement, with fresh staggered cooldowns on promotion. Ordinary Greens remain count-based. Upgraded Purples can summon into one row below the lowest occupied row, including the bottom game-ending row, but never outside the grid. Upgraded Yellows visually disguise themselves without connecting to the copied enemy or changing their true color; any non-Yellow ordinary hit reveals them with a flash without stopping the tongue, while Yellow and magic hits kill them as Yellow. Disguises do not contribute to copied-color groups or promotion. Enemy deaths cascade into colored digits by connection distance: an unlabeled first enemy, `2` at direct neighbors, `3` at the next ring. Each enemy's points are multiplied by its digit (the hidden first digit still means x1). Only chains of three or more earn multipliers. Magic shots add the preceding chain peak to local connection depths, so equally distant branches share multipliers and the next chain begins above the previous peak. Chains of one or two award base points and use unnumbered singleton pops without advancing or resetting the magic peak. Defeat counts and fleet-clear bonuses are unchanged. The replaceable presentation preserves immediate combat removal and scoring.

Purple cooldowns currently start at 6-9 seconds and Yellow at 1.5-4.5. Special Greens (and ordinary Greens under the optional legacy dash switch) use 6-15-second cooldowns and a 0.6-second warning before a 0.1-unit dash over 0.18 seconds. Every level adds 3% of the initial fleet speed (up to twice that speed), and reduces maximum ability cooldowns by 0.25 seconds, preserving a range of at least three seconds above each unchanged minimum. Existing countdowns are not shortened mid-cycle.

Clearing the last enemy of a color grants a magic shot, with up to two charges stored. The player changes color every 0.1 seconds when magic is ready or firing; the level-progress fill follows the player's displayed color. The next shot is a rainbow piercing tongue that hits any color, bypasses shields, and clears each hit enemy's same-color chain. It continues to full reach unless it clears the fleet, then visibly retracts. Misses spend the charge; blocked fire commands do not. Getting hit, clearing the fleet, or restarting resets magic, as in the original. The original color-wheel visual direction is intentionally replaced by the bottom color-clear blocks.

Cleared colors cannot appear in new rows or Purple summons while their color bars are visible. Colored segments at the bottom track consecutive color-clearing shots. A shot that clears no entire color removes all segments when it returns (or is canceled), releasing those colors' spawn locks without removing magic rewards. Returning colors can be cleared again to earn their bars and rewards anew. Each slot occupies 1/N of the full progress-track width, where N is the number of distinct colors actually seen in the current fleet, including colors already cleared. Unlocks and override selections alone do not add slots. Seen history resets with each new batch. Override changes cannot bypass active color locks. If only locked colors are selected while enemies remain, the fleet still descends but adds no new row.

Magic shots fire immediately after accepted input and extend/retract four times as fast as normal shots (56/80 world units per second). They remain twice as wide. The wind-up and charge animation are no longer used. The player can move during the shot, repeated fire is blocked, and a hit or game over cancels the active shot. On the final enemy hit, both kinds of tongue visibly retract instead of disappearing. The refill waits for the tongue to return. The player then switches directly to a color guaranteed to appear in the already-planned next batch, without a grey state. When multiple colors will spawn, a different color is preferred. Override edits during the pause affect later spawns, not that reserved batch.

Missile art is replaceable in `Assets/Prefabs/Red Missile.prefab`; shield art is assigned on **Enemy Grid**. The full checks cover shield hit ordering, recharge, matching bypass, projectile cleanup, swept player hits, cancelled shots, respawning, and protection expiry, including live Play mode behavior.

Disconnected enemies retreat upward as groups, one cell every 0.15 seconds, until they reconnect to the fleet anchored at the top row. Horizontal and vertical links count across all colors; diagonals do not. Groups keep their shape and are not destroyed by retreat. If the top row is empty, survivors rise to it. Adjust **Retreat Step Seconds** on **Enemy Grid** to change the pace. Logical cells and visuals move together, so matching and descent use the updated positions. Automated checks cover connectivity, shape preservation, color counts, timing, and reconnection after a connecting enemy is cleared in Play mode.

Art is reused from the original color-chameleon project. Replace Sprite Renderer sprites on the five color-named enemy prefabs in `Assets/Prefabs` to update enemy art independently of movement. Ability cues are isolated in `EnemyPresentation`. Camera framing keeps the background solid black and preserves the formation on portrait and landscape screens.

Run the editor checks from **Candy Cruisers > Run Checks** with Play mode stopped and scene changes saved. From the command line, launch Unity with `-batchmode -projectPath <project-folder> -executeMethod CandyCruisers.Editor.GameplayChecks.Run -quit -logFile <log-path>`. These checks cover grid occupancy, neighbors, color changes, wrapping, gesture classification, tongue locking/reset, movement timing, scene references, and camera framing. They render portrait, landscape, tall-phone, and tongue previews into `TestResults`.

The full suite also includes a separate Play mode check: launch Unity with `-batchmode -projectPath <project-folder> -executeMethod CandyCruisers.Editor.RuntimeGameplayChecks.Run -logFile <log-path>` (without `-quit`; it exits after testing). This verifies scene startup, enemy disable/enable registration, moving fleet, and tongue extension/return/refire during actual frame updates. Close the editor before running either batch command. Touch logic is covered by automated checks; physical-device feel still needs a phone playtest.
